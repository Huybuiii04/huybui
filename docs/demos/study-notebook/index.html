The little library
that grew

When Mara moved to Fenwick in 2019, she found the town library inside a former railway station. The trains had stopped coming many years earlier, but the building still brought people together. Every morning, readers gathered at its long wooden tables. Membership and all library activities were free.

At first, Mara came only to borrow novels. Then she noticed a handwritten sign asking for volunteers. She offered to help repair damaged books. Soon, she was spending two afternoons a week at a small desk near the window. Visitors often stopped to ask questions, and she enjoyed hearing their stories.

In 2021, the team started a seed library. People could take a small packet of seeds, grow vegetables at home, and bring back fresh seeds at the end of the season. The project began with just twelve packets in an old drawer. Local gardeners later donated enough seeds to fill an entire cabinet.

The library now holds workshops every Saturday. Some visitors learn how to look after plants, while others practise simple book repairs. Volunteers organise these sessions. They choose activities after talking to local residents, rather than following a fixed national programme.

For Mara, the most useful change is not the number of books or seeds. It is the way people greet each other. A visitor who first arrives looking for a quiet place to read may eventually become someone who helps a neighbour learn something new.
